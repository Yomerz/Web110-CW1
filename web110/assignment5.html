The next topic I am describing is Smart Insights: The Honeycomb model and evaluating the different social media platforms. I have found that pretty much all of them operate within the honeycomb model. There are some exceptions, such as not being able to have conversations on Pinterest, as it’s mostly for just sharing creative work, and in the same “comb” of conversation, LinkedIn also falls in there due to conversations being mainly professionally focused. Otherwise, the rest of the contenders apply the model really well, and more importantly, in their own unique way. As a UX Designer, this is important to study to be able to see what has people coming back, what works, and what doesn’t. As the landscape of social networking evolves, new networks and apps are naturally going to come out, and it would be nice to maybe work for a startup and help them with design that then later gets recognized for its usefulness and use of UX/UI. Also, as a developer, it’s important to incorporate the honeycomb method if I myself am ever planning on developing such a platform. There’s a reason that the companies that follow these standards the best are the best themselves, and I would be stupid not to take note.
