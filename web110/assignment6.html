The topic I am describing is VoIP/Messaging, reviewing and completing the tutorials for the different communication applications. It is interesting to see all the different communication applications, since somewhere around 2020 Zoom kind of became the universal standard. I am sure more competitors will arise in the future; however, seeing as this was also my first time hearing about Google Meet, and Google being the company it is, it is highly unlikely. It is important to learn from this because Zoom blends ease of use, integration with other applications, and a clean, easy-to-understand user interface. WhatsApp, from personal experience, is more common among foreigners and immigrants for easy communication with family back home, but it is being adopted more in America as of late to avoid international call costs. Another communication application that I like to use is Viber. The reason people use these applications and their widespread popularity is that as more and more people have access to technology, they in turn have access to these applications, and what typically wins is whether your grandma can operate it. The more users, the more connectivity, the more popular your application becomes, gaining value before you decide to sell it off; it's a self-feeding loop. And as a future UX designer, I plan to study the design cues of popular applications to be able to improve and simplify the experience of the application I am working on. The next topic I am describing is Screen Sharing/Remote Desktop. I have always found these types of applications to be cool, as when I was younger I thought you needed to be an immensely skilled hacker to be able to do that. However, these types of programs are very common everywhere for both professional and personal use cases. It is good to be able to know these screen-sharing applications, as they come in very handy. Recently, when my grandfather went back to Ukraine, he used a software program I was not familiar with, but it was a screen-sharing software that allowed him to connect to the desktop at his house in Chicago from his laptop in Ukraine, and he was able to take care of business. In the future, as a UX designer, there might be times when I am away from the office, where I could potentially be storing the main files, and I would need to access them for a client; it would be important to have this already set up to be able to work from any location. As for another use that I experienced with screen-sharing programs is that when I used to work for a trucking company, our boss had an obsession with being able to look at everyone's screen in the company at the same time and also through CCTV footage. It was definitely an interesting time, and I am happy to be done with that. The next topic I am describing is Intellectual Property. After reviewing the RIAA policy, it is important to follow the rules, especially since serious cases can carry jail time and involve very heavy fines. Piracy is not a fun thing and has been a problem since the internet was conceived. It is morally wrong to steal and distribute something that you don’t rightfully own. Loss of profit, leaking of sensitive information, and mental strain are all negative effects of this act. If it were to happen to you, it would be devastating. It is important to also copyright your own work so that other people can’t just steal it and pass it off as their own. Over the years, there have been lots of copyright and IP battles, one of the more famous ones being Spider-Man in limbo between Sony and Marvel. As a UX designer, it is critical that I come up with original ideas and designs and don’t just rip others off, because not only is that corny and involves no skill, but I can be sent to jail too.
