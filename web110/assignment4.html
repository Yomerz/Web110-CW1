The topic I am describing is sending, receiving, and responding to email. In this activity, although basic, it refreshed a lot of skills that are useful to have in email. Things like creating groups and filtering emails to make it quicker to search for specific information or talk with specific people were a good refresher to have. These skills are becoming ever more important in the growing age of technology, as everything is moved from analog to digital, and with the popularity of Gmail, it is essential to have these skills. As a UX Designer and just a general human on planet Earth, it is important to be able to navigate your email on your preferred browser in order to pay your bills, receive and send important information, and communicate for work. In my line of work in the future as a UX Designer, I will be using email to communicate projects, sending and receiving emails back and forth with clients, and it is important to not mix them up and retain proper netiquette so they can reference me in the future. The next topic I am describing is viewing email message headers. In this activity, we were taught how to examine full message headers within our preferred browser in order to identify the sender to see if it was a spoofed email. Nowadays, Google and similar providers have pretty good detection for these sorts of things, but it is always good to know how to identify and be able to check for yourself. This is useful for both personal and professional purposes. For personal use, it is good to be vigilant so that you don’t install any malware or ransomware on your computer through a spoofed email; this may cause emotional, financial, and other problems in someone's life. For professional purposes, the reasons are more or less the same except for the addition of making sure you don't give out proprietary information to unknown users for possibly malicious intentions. When working for a company, I will make sure to double-check everything that comes across my email to make sure that safety and security is upheld and there are no leaks. The next topic I am describing is using Internet mailing lists. Mailing lists are a good way to stay up to date on your favorite topics. As a student of UX design, it would be in my interest to subscribe to ux related mailing lists in order for me to keep up with current information and technology coming out in the field. It’s nice to be able to brew up a hot cup of tea in the morning and go through a condensed list of information rather than individually looking up each topic yourself; it’s time-efficient. Mailing lists could also be made as a hobby. An old acquaintance with whom I used to play hockey back in high school has recently started his own mailing list in which he writes articles based on his opinions about certain topics about the NHL. I am not much of a sports follower now as I was back in the day, but I do support his efforts and think what he is doing is something unique. Mailing lists, in my opinion, will be replaced by AI summaries in the future; however, if enough people still subscribe to mailing lists because they enjoy the human aspect, maybe they will stick around.
