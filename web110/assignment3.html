The next topic I am describing is Comparing Search Engines. I have been using Google Chrome for so long that I forgot others even existed. And after looking up current market share, I am not surprised that Google is leading the race by a long shot. It could just be that they were the most recognizable due to marketing, and everyone just naturally floated towards them, but after looking at the competitors its clear to see why. I queried the search “best motorcycles pre tech era” in each of the following search engines: Google, Bing, Yahoo, and Ask.com. Funny enough, Ask.com turned out to be a case of link rot, except for the fact that they have a goodbye message posted on the site when you try to access it: RIP-Ask.com - June,1996 - May, 2026. As for the other competitors, Google still comes out on top; their UI just feels so much cleaner. Once you get past all of the AI stuff that every search engine seems to have incorporated nowadays and is pretty much similar, Google has a way nicer layout. The logos of the websites that make them more recognizable appear nicer and more legible on Chrome than on the others. Another fact is that Chrome offers a variety of different media right away- videos, forums, pictures; the other ones just don’t do it as cleanly or with a nice layout as Chrome does. This could be one of two reasons: one is that Chrome is just superior, the other being that, due to Chrome having 90% of the traffic, websites optimize primarily for Chrome and are not really paying attention to the other search engines as much. As a UX Designer, this is important to note because if I want to increase usability, it would make sense to optimize for all engines; however, would the company, for example, want to spend money on development for the specialization of other search engines that are less than 5% of the market share?
