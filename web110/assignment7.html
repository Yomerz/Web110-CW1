The next topic I am describing is researching and comparing license and support costs for Windows, macOS, and Linux. This matters as a UX designer because different industries have different operating-system preferences. In the design industry, people often use Apple devices; since the OS comes free with a Mac, for example, everyone with an Apple device gets the latest version of the operating system with maximum security at no extra cost, as long as they don’t jailbreak it. In the world of UX, Windows is preferred more for the backend. Its customizability and access to more accessories support heavy coding and back-end structures. A Windows Pro license typically costs about $200 per user, and all updates afterward are included for free. Most businesses opt for the business plan, where accounts are given to everyone in the organization and kept under one umbrella for the corporation. This saves money compared with giving each workstation its own plan. Linux, on the other hand, is an operating system preferred by (no offense) tech geeks because of its extreme customizability and ability to highly personalize your computer; it's also extremely popular among serious coders, infrastructure architects, cybersecurity personnel, and the like. In my case, as a UX designer, I think I will stick with Apple for now, since it is the industry standard.
